FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Educational guidance for Indian medical aspirants, prospective students, and parents regarding medical college academics and procedures.

The Bachelor of Medicine and Bachelor of Surgery (MBBS) is the primary undergraduate medical degree awarded to graduates in India. It prepares students with comprehensive theoretical knowledge, laboratory skills, and supervised clinical training across medical, surgical, and diagnostic disciplines.
General requirements typically include passing 10+2 or equivalent higher secondary examination with Physics, Chemistry, Biology/Biotechnology, and English as core subjects, obtaining the minimum qualifying aggregate marks as prescribed by statutory authorities, and meeting the applicable age criteria. Candidates must also qualify for the designated national entrance examination.
Yes. Under prevailing Indian regulations, qualifying in the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET-UG) conducted by designated testing authorities is the mandatory single entrance gateway for admission to undergraduate medical programs across all recognized institutions in India.
An MBBS curriculum in India spans 4.5 academic years divided into Pre-Clinical, Para-Clinical, and Clinical professional phases, followed by a mandatory 1-year Compulsory Rotating Medical Internship (CRMI), bringing the total duration to 5.5 years.
After completing MBBS and the mandatory internship, doctors can pursue postgraduate degrees such as Doctor of Medicine (MD) in non-surgical specialties (e.g., General Medicine, Paediatrics, Pathology) or Master of Surgery (MS) in surgical disciplines (e.g., General Surgery, Orthopaedics, Ophthalmology) through designated national postgraduate examinations.
The curriculum encompasses Pre-Clinical subjects (Human Anatomy, Physiology, Biochemistry), Para-Clinical subjects (Pathology, Pharmacology, Microbiology, Forensic Medicine, Community Medicine), and Clinical disciplines (General Medicine, General Surgery, Obstetrics & Gynaecology, Paediatrics, Orthopaedics, Ophthalmology, ENT, Dermatology, Psychiatry).
Clinical training is supervised practical education where medical students participate in patient history taking, physical examination demonstrations, ward rounds, case discussions, diagnostic evaluations, and procedural observation under the mentorship of senior physicians and surgeons.
Graduates can practice clinical medicine as medical officers, pursue postgraduate super-specializations (DM/MCh), engage in medical research and public health initiatives, teach in medical academia, or contribute to healthcare administration and policy development.
Students apply by qualifying in NEET, registering for centralized state or national counselling sessions conducted by authorized government counselling committees, selecting preferred choices based on merit, and completing formal document verification at the allotted institution.
